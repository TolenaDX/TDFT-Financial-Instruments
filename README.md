
# TDFT — Digital Financial Instruments

### Ten Programmable Financial Instruments for Global Trade Finance

**Tolena Digital Technology**

TDFT (Tolena Digital Financial Technology) is a blockchain-native financial infrastructure designed to support programmable financial instruments, conditional financial obligations, and institutional trade-finance operations.

The TDFT portfolio comprises ten planned digital financial instruments, designed to address different areas of international trade, commercial finance, guarantees, credit arrangements, and contractual settlement.

The platform combines structured financial workflows, defined contractual conditions, independent verification where applicable, and controlled settlement execution.

TDFT is designed for businesses, banks, financial institutions, and other authorized participants in global trade.

---

## 1. The Ten-Instrument Portfolio

TDFT is structured around a portfolio of ten digital financial instruments.

Three instruments have reached an advanced stage of technical development:

1. **DCC — Digital Credit Contract**
2. **DGI — Digital Guarantee Instrument**
3. **DSC — Digital Standby Credit**

DCC has successfully completed internal end-to-end validation.

DGI and DSC have completed their initial technical development and are undergoing further validation and deployment preparation.

Seven additional instruments form part of the broader TDFT development roadmap.

The instruments are intended to provide a common technological foundation for different categories of financial obligations while preserving the distinct contractual and operational requirements of each instrument.

---

## 2. DCC — Digital Credit Contract

### Programmable Trade-Finance Credit Instrument

DCC is a blockchain-native financial instrument designed to provide an alternative operational framework to traditional documentary letters of credit.

It supports conditional financial commitments, contractual performance verification, and controlled settlement execution within a structured digital environment.

### Principal Capabilities

DCC is designed to support:

- Digital creation of contractual financial commitments
- Acceptance by designated transaction participants
- Conditional allocation of settlement funds
- Independent verification of contractual performance
- Controlled authorization of settlement outcomes
- Contractually defined payment and refund conditions
- Milestone-based settlement where applicable
- Transaction monitoring and execution records

### Institutional Applications

DCC is intended for:

- International merchandise trade
- Import and export transactions
- Documentary trade-finance arrangements
- Conditional commercial payments
- Cross-border contractual settlement
- Milestone-based commercial obligations

DCC is the first TDFT instrument prioritized for institutional commercial introduction.

### Development and Validation Status

DCC has successfully completed internal end-to-end validation involving:

**Blockchain Networks**
- BNB Smart Chain
- Arc

**Settlement Assets**
- USDC
- EURC

The internal validation covered contractual workflows, participant interactions, conditional settlement processes, and transaction execution.

Arc integration has been implemented and internally validated on Arc Testnet.

The validation results demonstrate technical functionality within the tested environments. They do not constitute independent security certification, regulatory approval, or confirmation of production deployment.

Additional scenario testing, assurance activities, and institutional deployment preparations remain part of the production-readiness process.

DGI is a digital financial instrument designed to support conditional financial guarantee obligations through structured contractual workflows.

It is intended to provide a programmable operational framework for financial guarantees used in commercial and institutional transactions.

### Principal Capabilities

DGI is designed to support:

- Digitally managed guarantee obligations
- Defined contractual conditions
- Authorized participant responsibilities
- Conditional guarantee execution
- Controlled transaction procedures
- Transparent operational records
- Institutional monitoring and oversight

### Potential Applications

DGI is intended for commercial and institutional use cases involving financial guarantees, subject to the applicable contractual arrangements and regulatory requirements.

### Development Status

DGI has completed its initial technical development and reached an advanced stage of internal validation.

Further testing, assurance, and deployment preparation will precede commercial introduction.

DGI is planned for introduction following the initial DCC commercial rollout.

## 6. Common Infrastructure Principles

Although the ten instruments address different financial functions, TDFT is designed around common infrastructure principles.

These include:

- Programmable contractual conditions
- Defined participant responsibilities
- Authorized transaction actions
- Conditional financial execution
- Transparent transaction records
- Institutional operational oversight
- Configurable financial workflows
- Integration with supported settlement infrastructure

Each instrument is intended to retain the contractual and operational characteristics appropriate to its financial purpose.

The specific implementation of these principles may differ between instruments.

---

## 7. Conditional Financial Execution

Conditional execution is a central concept within TDFT.

Financial actions are designed to occur according to predefined contractual requirements and authorized procedures.

Depending on the instrument, these may involve:

- Acceptance of contractual obligations
- Confirmation of funding arrangements
- Verification of agreed performance conditions
- Authorization of permitted financial outcomes
- Contractual milestones
- Expiration conditions
- Settlement or other contractually permitted actions

The objective is to improve execution predictability and operational transparency while maintaining clear contractual responsibilities.

Not all financial instruments follow identical settlement or verification procedures.

---

## 8. Independent Verification and Institutional Accountability

Certain TDFT financial instruments, particularly DCC, incorporate independent verification as part of their contractual workflow.

The purpose is to separate assessment of contractual performance from the authority to execute financial settlement.

This approach is designed to support:

- Clear participant responsibilities
- Independent assessment where required
- Controlled execution permissions
- Reduced reliance on unilateral transaction decisions
- Transparent contractual accountability

Verification and settlement responsibilities are determined by the applicable instrument and contractual arrangements.
